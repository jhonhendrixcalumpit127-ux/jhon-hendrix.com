Why Digital Skills Matter

Introduction

In today’s world, digital skills are no longer optional—they are essential for almost every part of life. From schoolwork and communication to future careers, knowing how to use technology effectively helps us succeed. In Empowerment Technologies, we learn that digital literacy gives students the power to create, collaborate, and solve problems in new ways.

Empowering Students

Digital skills allow students to access information quickly, organize their work better, and express their ideas creatively. Tools like spreadsheets help us analyze data, while web development and design platforms let us build our own projects. These abilities make learning more interactive and prepare us for the demands of the modern world.

Connecting and Creating

Technology connects people across distances. Through online platforms, we can share our work, collaborate on projects, and learn from others. Digital skills also encourage creativity—whether we are designing graphics, writing code, or creating presentations. When we master these tools, we turn ideas into real outputs that others can see and use.

Preparing for the Future

Many jobs now require at least basic digital skills. By learning them early, we become more confident and competitive. More importantly, these skills teach us to adapt. Technology keeps changing, and students who understand how to learn new tools will always be ready for whatever comes next.

Conclusion

Digital skills matter because they empower us to learn better, create more, and connect with the world. In EmpTech and beyond, these abilities are not just about using computers—they are about building a stronger, more capable version of ourselves.

Reflection (Sample Answers)

1. Most used formula:

'=C4*D4 (Total Sales) and =F4-D4 (Remaining Stock)

2. Formula vs Function:

Formula is any calculation (e.g. =A1*B1). Function is a predefined formula (SUM, AVERAGE, MAX).

3. Why cell references?

If data changes, formulas update automatically. Manual numbers require retyping everything.

4. Sorting & filtering large data:

Quickly identify top/bottom performers and focus on relevant subsets without scanning all rows.

5. Why charts useful?

Visual comparison makes trends and differences obvious at a glance for better decision-making.

6. Problems from incorrect data:

Wrong totals, bad decisions on restocking, inventory shortages or overstock, lost revenue.

7. Real business application:

Any retail store can track sales, monitor stock levels, identify best-sellers, and plan purchasing.

Notes

  • To sort: Select A3:H13 → Data → Sort → Sort by Total Sales (Largest to Smallest)
  • To filter: Select A3:H13 → Data → Filter. Then use dropdowns for Category, Quantity Sold >10, Remaining Stock <10
  • After filtering, clear the filter to restore full dataset.
